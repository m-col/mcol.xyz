      1 rewritefs: take back control over your dotfiles
      2 ===============================================
      3 
      4 :date: 2019-08-17
      5 :summary: take back control over your home folder dotfiles with rewritefs
      6 
      7 Many of us have had the unfortunate inevitability of using a program written by
      8 a sadistic programmer who sticks their program's files right into your neatly
      9 organised home folder. Fortunately, some rebels against the
     10 what-are-`XDG-directory-standards
     11 <https://standards.freedesktop.org/basedir-spec/basedir-spec-latest.html>`_
     12 cabal have come up with clever solutions to force all of your hidden
     13 files to stay within the bounds of your .config folder.
     14 
     15 Inspired by the likes of Apache and nginx, `rewritefs
     16 <https://github.com/sloonz/rewritefs>`_ uses arbitrary regex rules to rewrite
     17 (surprise surprise) paths accessed in a mount point. Using a `FUSE
     18 <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filesystem_in_Userspace>`_ approach, rewritefs
     19 simply mounts a user's a folder and the rest of the functionality is invisible.
     20 
     21 This example ruleset is provided to solve the problem of dotfiles clogging your
     22 home directory:
     23 
     24 .. code-block:: bbcbasic
     25 
     26     m#^(?!\.)# .
     27     m#^\.(cache|config|local)# .
     28     m#^\.# .config/
     29 
     30 We have one rule per line, rewriting strings matched by the first part to the
     31 content of the second part, with the special rule :code:`.` meaning 'do not
     32 rewrite'. E.g. :code:`.vimrc` becomes :code:`.config/vimrc`.
     33 
     34 To use these rules all we have to do is mount the directory, in this example
     35 our home directory from :code:`/mnt` into :code:`/home`, i.e.:
     36 
     37 .. code-block:: bash
     38 
     39     rewritefs -o config=/mnt/home/me/.config/rewritefs /mnt/home/me /home/me
     40 
     41 It's worth spending a bit of time going through all the junk in $HOME while
     42 setting up a ruleset, as many folders might be more appropriate going into
     43 :code:`.cache` or :code:`.local`.
     44 
     45 A massive plus of careful rulesetting and rewriting the appropriate files into
     46 :code:`.config` is that you can backup or version :code:`.config` in its
     47 entirety while putting things you don't care so much about into :code:`.cache`
     48 or elsewhere. This is much tidier than having to deal with scripting symlinks
     49 into :code:`$HOME` from your dotfiles repo, and more out-of-the-way than
     50 version tracking your home directory directly.
     51 
     52 Another nifty feature is the ability to specify rules on a per-application
     53 basis. For example, you could use this rule to make your annoying colleague's
     54 vim mysteriously replace the letter s for the letter z:
     55 
     56 .. code-block:: bbcbasic
     57 
     58     - /^\S*vim/
     59     /s/ z
     60 
     61 In such a rule, the line starting with - match the program name, and any
     62 following lines apply only to that scope.
     63 
     64 With great power comes great responsibility, so one must be careful with the
     65 power of rewritefs. Despite its power, however, there do seem to be only few
     66 real applications for such functionality. I would definitely be interested in
     67 hearing what other use people have made for this program.
